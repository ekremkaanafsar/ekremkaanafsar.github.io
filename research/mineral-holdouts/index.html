The question

Oil and gas drilling units bring many neighbouring properties together, and some owners hold out. This paper asks whether economic hold-out behaviour differs between conventional vertical wells and unconventional horizontal hydraulic fracturing, and whether holding out looks like sincere opposition or strategic rent-seeking.

Data

The evidence comes from 254 New York State compulsory pooling orders issued between 2004 and 2014, covering more than 4,600 individually coded properties. Every property record was coded individually and went through systematic verification and quality checks.

Key findings

01

Horizontal wells generate significantly more hold-out behaviour overall than conventional vertical wells.

02

Marginal, cross-boundary properties are significantly less likely to hold out. This pattern is consistent with sincere opposition rather than strategic rent-seeking.

My role

  • Managed the dataset of 4,600+ individually coded property records from the pooling orders, with systematic verification and quality checks.
  • Contributed GIS-coded well-unit mapping.
  • I run all of the statistical analyses in Stata and R.
